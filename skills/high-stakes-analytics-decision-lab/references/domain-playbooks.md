# Domain Playbooks

## Health and biostatistics

Consider health outcomes, adverse events, access gaps, sample uncertainty, missingness, transportability, and budget impact. Distinguish prognostic prediction from treatment-effect estimation. Require clinical and ethics review before deployment.

For study-design decisions, define the estimand, assignment mechanism, analysis
population, censoring, multiplicity, missing-data strategy, and subgroup plan.
Use the evidence module only for transparent introductory estimates; complex
trials require design-specific statistical software and review.

## Public and technology policy

Consider statutory authority, administrative capacity, distributional effects, take-up, implementation delay, rights, public legitimacy, and reversibility. Include a status quo and at least one nontechnical intervention.

## Finance and risk

Consider expected return, capital usage, liquidity, credit loss, operational risk, compliance, model risk, and tail loss. Replace the illustrative factor loadings with evidence-backed credit, liquidity, market, or funding dependencies and review the independent/declared/stressed comparison. Never present the examples as investment advice.

For corporate finance and accounting, reconcile net present value with free
cash flow, return on invested capital, leverage, cash coverage, tax,
capitalization, covenant, and reporting-policy assumptions.

## Business and marketing analytics

Consider incremental value rather than raw prediction, acquisition and retention effects, cannibalization, brand or consumer harm, operational capacity, and experimentation cost. Avoid optimizing only a proxy metric.

## Engineering systems and operations

Consider reliability, resilience, throughput, lifecycle cost, safety, recovery time, and supplier or network dependencies. Represent non-negotiable safety requirements as constraints.

Use exact grid optimization only when discretization is decision-relevant and
the search space is small. State the grid, coefficients, constraint units,
scenario values, and solver boundary.

## Urban planning and regeneration

Consider accessibility, displacement, housing affordability, environmental impact, fiscal feasibility, spatial spillovers, and implementation time. Aggregate results can hide neighborhood-level harms.

## Behavioral and decision science

Consider framing, default effects, present bias, autonomy, heterogeneous treatment effects, experiment design, and ethical acceptability. Separate observed preferences from welfare.

## AI governance and ethics

Consider task validity, data representativeness, calibration, error asymmetry, human oversight, contestability, privacy, security, group harms, and fallback procedures. Quantitative parity metrics do not resolve normative disagreement.

For technical model validation, review out-of-sample construction, leakage,
discrimination, calibration, threshold costs, subgroup sample sizes, drift,
human-review capacity, monitoring, and rollback. Predictive quality alone does
not authorize deployment.
